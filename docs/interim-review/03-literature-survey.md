# (3) Literature Survey

**Status:** Approved  
**Word limit:** 500–600 words  
**Word count:** 550  
**Base paper:** Soppari et al., *Forensic sketch generation using Gen-AI*, WJARR, 2026 (`papers/Direct_Ppaper/`)  
**Related papers:** IEEE / Elsevier / Research-GATE folders (concept papers treated as supporting algorithms only)

---

The present survey is organized around related forensic and text-to-face research papers available in this repository. Foundational algorithm papers (StyleGAN2, ArcFace, FaceNet, Attention) are treated only as supporting building blocks; the design baseline is the direct Gen-AI forensic sketch paper and the related application studies below.

**Base paper — Forensic sketch generation using Gen-AI (Soppari et al., 2026).** This work proposes an end-to-end forensic pipeline: eyewitness text is cleaned and turned into a prompt; Stable Diffusion XL generates a forensic-style face; InsightFace and BiSeNet extract biometric and semantic features; FAISS performs hybrid mugshot retrieval. It demonstrates that text-to-sketch generation can be linked directly to suspect ranking, reducing artist dependency. Its own stated future work—iterative sketch refinement, stronger multimodal fusion, and richer marks such as scars—defines the research space our project extends. We therefore treat this paper as the **primary base design reference**: same forensic goal (description → sketch → identity support), with a modular architecture and explicit witness-refinement loop as our differentiation.

**Text-to-face generation.** Text2FaceGAN (Nasir et al.) generates faces from CelebA-derived captions using DCGAN, but only at low resolution (\(64 \times 64\)) and with limited identity detail. Chen et al. (FTGAN) jointly train a text encoder and image decoder for fine-grained text-to-face synthesis and introduce SCU-Text2face, reporting about 59% average similarity to ground truth. Khan et al. (IEEE Access) likewise advocate fully trained GANs over frozen text encoders so word-level facial cues are not lost, and build an amalgamated labeled face–text dataset for public-safety use. TediGAN (Xia et al.) maps text into a pre-trained StyleGAN latent space for high-resolution (\(1024 \times 1024\)) controllable faces and multi-modal CelebA-HQ inputs, yet still inherits GAN instability and imperfect identity control. ST\(^2\)FG (Oza et al.) uses BERT encoding and iterative user refinement—closest psychologically to a forensic interview—via an Affine Combination Module, but remains GAN-bound. Together these works justify Module 1–3 of our system: structured language understanding, attribute-to-latent mapping, and high-fidelity face synthesis.

**Sketch–photo forensic transformation.** A large body of law-enforcement research assumes a sketch already exists and focuses on sketch-to-photo conversion for mugshot matching. Bushra and Maheswari review DCGAN-based forensic sketch-to-face transformation for crime investigation. Devakumar and Sarath (Elsevier) convert pencil sketches to real images with DCGAN and report improved SSIM versus prior methods. Kazemi et al. propose attribute-conditioned CycleGAN for unpaired sketch-to-photo synthesis with controllable traits such as hair/skin color. Li et al. address few-shot sketch styles via global–local asymmetric translation (GLAS). Yu et al. improve face sketch recognition through adversarial sketch–photo transformation so existing photo recognizers can be reused. CLIP4Sketch (Jain et al.) strengthens sketch–mugshot matching with diffusion-synthesized training data and multi-modal embeddings. These studies confirm identity-preserving cross-domain translation is essential—but they largely start after an artist (or software operator) has already produced the sketch.

**Synthesis for our design.** Prior related work therefore splits into (i) text-to-face generators without a full forensic sketch + matching loop, and (ii) sketch-to-photo / recognition systems that still need a hand-made composite. The base Gen-AI paper closes that loop with diffusion + FAISS matching, yet leaves iterative witness refinement and modular attribute editing as open enhancements. Our research adopts that forensic objective and redesigns the pipeline as attribute parsing → latent face generation → forensic sketch rendering → biometric evaluation with iterative feedback, aligning AI modules to real composite practice rather than one-shot prompting alone.
